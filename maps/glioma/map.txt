# Guideline set (Jonan 2026-11-19 11:34 UTC) and tag forms, grade as printed, on the statement the guideline graded.
#   Tag order on every line: EANO, NCCN, ASCO. Where clauses carry different tags, the EANO clause comes first
#   〔EANO class, level〕 EANO guideline on diffuse gliomas of adulthood (Weller, Nat Rev Clin Oncol 2021;18:170-186,
#     PMID 33293629), printed "C: II, L: B" (class of evidence I to IV, level of recommendation A to C). A bare 〔EANO〕
#     marks the text, Box 1 or Table 2, a sub-line whose grade sits on the line above, and the abstract and text of the
#     molecular testing guideline Update 1 (van den Bent, Neuro Oncol 2025;27:331-337, PMID 39387386). 〔EANO ESCAT
#     tier〕 is the tier Update 1 prints for a drug target, written hyphenated (Update 1 prints both forms). The 2021 guideline follows WHO 2016 with cIMPACT-NOW and
#     predates INDIGO, the CATNON 2021 and 2026 analyses and RTOG 1205. Its Figure 2 and
#     reference list are unread (notes-eano.md)
#   〔NCCN category〕 NCCN Central Nervous System Cancers 4.2026 (2 November 2026), algorithm pages GLIO-2 to GLIO-13.
#     2A unless stated. The preference label of the systemic therapy tables (GLIO-A) is printed too. A bare 〔NCCN〕
#     marks a footnote, a principles page (BRAIN-B, BRAIN-C, BRAIN-E) or the Discussion. The glioblastoma Discussion
#     dates from 2022. It still calls regorafenib a preferred option, while the current tables carry no regorafenib,
#     so no NCCN tag stands on the regorafenib line. The combination of lomustine with temozolomide was removed in
#     version 1.2026. Category 1 for vorasidenib is printed in GLIO-A for residual or measurable disease. The Discussion
#     alone cites the CATNON final analysis, and calls radiotherapy then temozolomide category 1 in grade 3 astrocytoma,
#     while GLIO-5 and GLIO-A print 2A (the map prints the algorithm, review question 3)
#   〔ASCO quality, strength〕 ASCO-SNO guideline 2022 (Mohile, J Clin Oncol 2022;40:403-426, PMID 34898238) with the
#     rapid update of 2025 on vorasidenib (Blakeley, J Clin Oncol 2025;43:2129-2133, PMID 40300117), which reprints
#     every "weak" grade as "conditional". 〔ASCO Strong〕 alone marks the recommendation for trial referral at recurrence,
#     printed "Evidence quality: no evidence considered". A bare 〔ASCO〕 marks the text, a qualifying statement,
#     Table 2 (doses) or the two "no recommendation" statements. It predates the CATNON final analysis
# Licence notes (checked 2026-11-19). A licence sits on its own ・ line, tagged only where a guideline prints it:
#   Vorasidenib: FDA 6 August 2024 as printed by ASCO. EU marketing authorisation 17 September 2025 (EMA EPAR for
#     Voranigo, read 2026-11-19): predominantly non-enhancing grade 2 astrocytoma or oligodendroglioma with an IDH1 R132
#     or IDH2 R172 mutation, from 12 years and 40 kg, surgery only, no immediate need for radiotherapy or chemotherapy.
#     Singapore status unknown
#   Bevacizumab: licensed for recurrent glioblastoma in the United States (NCCN, ASCO) and not in the European Union
#     (EANO). Licensed in Japan for malignant glioma (PMDA package insert, revision of June 2026, read 2026-11-19),
#     approved 17 June 2013 for newly diagnosed glioblastoma with radiotherapy and temozolomide and for recurrent
#     disease (Roche announcement as reported by European Pharmaceutical Review). Japan counts as a licence
#     (inclusion.txt). The first-line option stays a ※ line, as ASCO advises against it and no guideline recommends it
#     beyond EANO's large symptomatic tumour (Jonan to confirm)
#   Carmustine wafer: FDA licence for newly diagnosed high-grade glioma and recurrent glioblastoma (ASCO text names the
#     first). Tumour-treating fields: FDA licence for newly diagnosed (2015) and recurrent glioblastoma (NCCN). Both
#     stay ※ lines at recurrence or at resection, as NCCN alone grades them there (2B) and EANO and ASCO report no
#     gain or make no statement
#   Dabrafenib with trametinib: tumour-agnostic FDA licence for BRAF V600E solid tumours after earlier treatment.
#     Larotrectinib and entrectinib: tumour-agnostic EU and FDA licences for NTRK fusions (EANO 2023), repotrectinib
#     FDA. Pembrolizumab: tumour-agnostic FDA licences for MSI-H or mismatch repair-deficient and for TMB-high solid

band: WHO 2021 diagnosis | flow
  ? Diffuse astrocytic glioma or oligodendroglioma on histology: IDH mutation status
    ‹IDH-mutant› ? Testing for 1p/19q codeletion, ATRX, TP53 and CDKN2A/B
      ‹1p/19q codeletion› ? Oligodendroglioma: grade by histology and CDKN2A/B status
        ‹Grade 2, CDKN2A/B not deleted› ■ Oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted, grade 2 | OLI
        ‹Grade 3, or homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion› ■ Oligodendroglioma, IDH-mutant and 1p/19q-codeleted, grade 3 | OLI
      ‹ATRX loss, TP53 mutation› ? Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant: microvascular proliferation or necrosis
        ‹No› ? Testing for CDKN2A/B
          ‹CDKN2A/B not deleted› ? Grade by histology
            ‹Grade 2› ■ Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, grade 2 | AST
            ‹Grade 3› ■ Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, grade 3 | AST
          ‹Homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion› ■ Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, grade 4 | AST
        ‹Yes› see: Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant, grade 4
    ‹IDH-wildtype› ? Microvascular proliferation or necrosis
      ‹Yes› see: Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype, grade 4
      ‹No› ? Testing for EGFR, TERT, chromosomes 7 and 10, H3 K27 and H3 G34
        ‹EGFR amplification, TERT promoter mutation, or chromosome 7 gain with chromosome 10 loss› ■ Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype, grade 4 | GBM
        ‹H3 G34 mutation› ■ Diffuse hemispheric glioma, H3 G34-mutant, grade 4
        ‹H3 K27 alteration› ■ Diffuse midline glioma, H3 K27-altered, grade 4
  ※ Glioblastoma on microvascular proliferation or necrosis assumes negative H3 K27M testing in a midline tumour and negative H3 G34 testing in a younger patient
  ※ An IDH-wildtype glioma with none of these alterations is a provisional astrocytoma, IDH-wildtype, not elsewhere classified, of uncertain behaviour
  ※ An H3 K27-altered glioma outside the midline does not meet the criteria for diffuse midline glioma
  ※ Diffuse hemispheric glioma, H3 G34-mutant: temozolomide chemoradiotherapy is a reasonable approach 〔EANO〕

band: Molecular testing
  ① Every diffuse glioma: immunohistochemistry for IDH1 R132H and for nuclear ATRX 〔EANO IV, B〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・Multigene panel testing is the preferred approach to the pathological work-up 〔NCCN〕
    ・Beyond IDH, BRAF V600E is the only target found by panel testing with a treatment of proven benefit 〔EANO〕
  ② Negative IDH1 R132H staining in a grade 2 or 3 tumour, patient under 55 years: sequencing of IDH1 codon 132 and IDH2 codon 172 〔EANO IV, B〕 〔NCCN〕 〔ASCO〕
    ・Sequencing extends to every grade 2 or 3 tumour 〔EANO〕, and to tumours with glioblastoma histology in patients under 55 years 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN〕
  ③ IDH-mutant glioma with retained ATRX: 1p/19q codeletion testing 〔EANO II, B〕 〔NCCN〕
  ④ IDH-mutant astrocytoma: testing for homozygous CDKN2A/B deletion 〔EANO IV, B〕 〔NCCN〕
  ⑤ IDH-wildtype diffuse glioma without necrosis or microvascular proliferation: testing for TERT promoter mutation, EGFR amplification and gain of chromosome 7 with loss of chromosome 10 〔EANO IV, B〕 〔NCCN〕
  ⑥ Glioblastoma, notably in older or frail patients: MGMT promoter methylation testing, which guides the use of temozolomide 〔EANO I, B〕 〔NCCN〕 〔ASCO〕
  ⑦ IDH-wildtype diffuse glioma: BRAF V600E testing can be considered 〔EANO IV, C〕 〔NCCN〕
  ⑧ Mismatch repair testing is considered in patients under 50 years, with unusual tumour features or with a suggestive personal or family history 〔EANO〕, and is recommended in newly diagnosed glioblastoma with a high tumour mutational burden (TMB-high) 〔NCCN〕
  ※ Mismatch repair-deficient or TMB-high glioma: responses to immune checkpoint inhibitors are rare, and treatment within a trial or registry is preferred at diagnosis 〔EANO ESCAT III-A〕 and after earlier treatment 〔EANO ESCAT III-B〕
    ・Pembrolizumab holds tumour-agnostic FDA licences for microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) or mismatch repair-deficient and for TMB-high solid tumours

band: Regimens
  ① Temozolomide chemoradiotherapy, 6-week course: 60 Gy in 30 fractions with concurrent temozolomide 75 mg/m² daily, then maintenance temozolomide 150 to 200 mg/m² on days 1 to 5 every 28 days for six cycles 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN〕 〔ASCO〕
  ② Short-course temozolomide chemoradiotherapy, 3-week course: 40.05 Gy in 15 fractions with concurrent temozolomide 75 mg/m² daily for 21 days, then maintenance temozolomide 150 to 200 mg/m² on days 1 to 5 every 28 days for up to 12 cycles 〔ASCO〕
  ③ Temozolomide alone in older patients: 100 mg/m² on days 1 to 7 every 14 days until progression, or 200 mg/m² on days 1 to 5 every 28 days for up to six cycles 〔ASCO〕
    ・The 5-day schedule continues until progression or for 12 months 〔EANO〕
  ④ Tumour-treating fields: worn for more than 18 hours a day, from the start of maintenance temozolomide until second progression 〔ASCO〕
  ⑤ Radiotherapy in IDH-mutant glioma: 50 to 54 Gy in grade 2 and 59.4 to 60 Gy in grade 3, in fractions of 1.8 to 2 Gy 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN〕 〔ASCO〕
    ・A dose as low as 45 Gy may be appropriate in grade 2 〔NCCN〕
  ⑥ PCV after radiotherapy: lomustine 110 mg/m² on day 1, procarbazine 60 mg/m² on days 8 to 21 and vincristine 1.4 mg/m² on days 8 and 29, every 8 weeks for six cycles 〔ASCO〕
    ・Carmustine may replace lomustine 〔NCCN〕
  ⑦ Radiotherapy followed by temozolomide in IDH-mutant glioma: maintenance temozolomide 150 to 200 mg/m² on days 1 to 5 every 28 days for 12 cycles 〔EANO〕 〔ASCO〕
  ⑧ Re-irradiation of recurrent glioblastoma: 35 Gy in 10 fractions 〔NCCN〕
  ⑨ Vorasidenib: 40 mg once daily, continuously
    ・Liver function is tested every 2 weeks for 2 months and monthly thereafter 〔ASCO〕
    ・Vorasidenib should not be offered during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or to patients of either sex seeking pregnancy 〔ASCO〕
  ° Toxicity of note: raised transaminases with vorasidenib, mainly reversible and asymptomatic 〔ASCO〕

course: Adult diffuse glioma | parallel
  Newly diagnosed diffuse glioma
    ① Maximal safe resection, or biopsy when resection is not safe 〔EANO IV, B〕 〔NCCN〕
      ・In IDH1-mutant tumours, resection of the whole T2 or FLAIR abnormality, and beyond it when safe, can be considered 〔NCCN〕
    ② MRI within 48 hours of surgery to document the extent of resection 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN〕
    ③ Radiotherapy to start 3 to 5 weeks after surgery 〔EANO〕
    ④ Eligible patients: a clinical trial where available 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
    ※ A carmustine wafer placed at resection of a grade 3 or 4 glioma is an option 〔NCCN 2B〕
      ・In the randomised trial in newly diagnosed grade 3 or 4 glioma, the survival difference with the wafer was not significant once grade 3 tumours were excluded 〔EANO〕
      ・The wafer holds an FDA licence for newly diagnosed grade 3 or 4 glioma 〔ASCO〕 and for recurrent glioblastoma

  Glioblastoma, IDH-wildtype: newly diagnosed
    branch: GBM
    ① Up to 70 years and fit, after resection or biopsy: temozolomide chemoradiotherapy 〔EANO I, A〕 〔NCCN 1〕 〔ASCO Moderate, Strong〕
      ・Temozolomide chemoradiotherapy is standard up to 70 years with a Karnofsky performance status (KPS) of 70 or more 〔EANO〕 〔ASCO〕, while a KPS of 60 or more suffices 〔NCCN〕
    + EORTC 22981/26981 {pmid 15758009}
    ② Up to 70 years and fit, MGMT promoter-unmethylated tumour: temozolomide chemoradiotherapy 〔NCCN 1〕 〔ASCO Moderate, Strong〕, or radiotherapy alone 〔NCCN 2A〕
      ・The activity of temozolomide is probably marginal in unmethylated tumours 〔EANO II, B〕
    ③ Older or frail, fit for temozolomide chemoradiotherapy but not for the 6-week course: short-course temozolomide chemoradiotherapy 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN 1〕 〔ASCO Moderate, Conditional〕
      ・The regimen is limited to MGMT promoter-methylated tumours 〔EANO〕, while unmethylated tumours remain eligible 〔NCCN 2A〕 〔ASCO〕
      ・Hypofractionation is appropriate above 65 to 70 years or with a KPS below 70 〔EANO〕, while no absolute cut-off of age or performance status is endorsed 〔ASCO〕
      ・Above 70 years with a KPS of 60 or more, the 6-week course with tumour-treating fields is an option 〔NCCN 1〕, as is the 6-week course alone 〔NCCN 2A〕
    + Perry et al. {pmid 28296618}
    ④ Older or frail, unsuited to temozolomide chemoradiotherapy, unmethylated tumour: hypofractionated radiotherapy alone, such as 40 Gy in 15 fractions 〔EANO II, B〕 〔NCCN 2A〕 〔ASCO Low, Conditional〕
      ・Unknown MGMT status is treated as unmethylated 〔EANO〕, while an indeterminate result is treated as methylated 〔NCCN〕
      ・Shorter schedules are 34 Gy in 10 fractions, and 25 Gy in 5 fractions for a smaller tumour in a patient who would not tolerate a longer course 〔NCCN〕
    ~ Roa et al. (2004) {pmid 15051755}
    ~ IAEA trial (Roa 2015) {pmid 26392096}
    ⑤ Older or frail, unsuited to temozolomide chemoradiotherapy, methylated tumour: temozolomide alone 〔EANO II, B〕 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕 〔ASCO Low, Conditional〕
    ~ NOA-08 {pmid 22578793}
    + Nordic {pmid 22877848}
    ⑥ Poor performance status with a large or multifocal tumour: best supportive care 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN 2A〕 〔ASCO Low, Conditional〕
      ・In patients 70 years or older with a KPS of 70 or more, radiotherapy prolonged survival over best supportive care 〔EANO〕 〔ASCO〕
    + Keime-Guibert et al. {pmid 17429084}
    ※ Lomustine added to temozolomide chemoradiotherapy improved overall survival in MGMT promoter-methylated glioblastoma in a small trial 〔EANO〕 〔ASCO〕
      + CeTeG/NOA-09 {pmid 30782343}
    ※ Bevacizumab added to temozolomide chemoradiotherapy is not recommended 〔ASCO Moderate, Conditional〕, as it did not improve overall survival in two trials 〔EANO〕 〔ASCO〕
      ・Bevacizumab could be useful in a patient with a large, highly symptomatic tumour who might not otherwise tolerate radiotherapy 〔EANO〕
      ・Bevacizumab is licensed in Japan for malignant glioma, including newly diagnosed glioblastoma with temozolomide chemoradiotherapy
      ~ AVAglio {pmid 24552318}
      - RTOG 0825 {pmid 24552317}
    ° Toxicity of note: myelosuppression with temozolomide, notably thrombocytopenia 〔EANO〕
    ° Toxicity of note: neurocognitive adverse events after 25 Gy in 5 fractions in patients with longer survival 〔EANO〕

  Astrocytoma, IDH-mutant
    branch: AST
    ① Grade 2, gross total resection without measurable disease: observation 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN 2A〕 〔ASCO Low, Conditional〕, or vorasidenib 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
      ・Observation is reserved for patients under about 40 years 〔EANO〕 〔ASCO〕 with no symptoms other than seizures 〔EANO〕
      ・Grade 2 astrocytoma progresses faster than grade 2 oligodendroglioma, so vorasidenib after gross total resection may also be reasonable 〔NCCN〕
      ・The uncertain benefit of starting vorasidenib at once is weighed against observation until measurable tumour appears 〔ASCO〕
    ※ Early radiotherapy prolonged progression-free survival, but not overall survival, compared with radiotherapy at progression 〔EANO〕 〔NCCN〕 〔ASCO〕
      ~ EORTC 22845 {pmid 16168780}
    ② Grade 2, residual disease or recurrence after surgery alone, radiotherapy and chemotherapy deferred: vorasidenib 〔EANO ESCAT I-A〕 〔NCCN 1, preferred〕 〔ASCO High, Conditional〕, or observation 〔NCCN 2A〕 〔ASCO Low, Conditional〕
      ・Ivosidenib is an alternative in IDH1-mutant tumours when vorasidenib is not tolerated 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
      ・Vorasidenib is licensed by the FDA from 12 years of age after biopsy or any resection 〔ASCO〕, and in the EU from 40 kg for a predominantly non-enhancing tumour treated by surgery alone, when radiotherapy or chemotherapy is not needed at once
    ~ INDIGO {pmid 37272516}
    ※ Data on vorasidenib are inadequate with measurable or nodular enhancement, uncontrolled seizures or brainstem involvement 〔ASCO〕
      ・INDIGO enrolled patients from 12 years of age 1 to 5 years after surgery alone, with measurable non-enhancing disease 〔NCCN〕 〔ASCO〕
