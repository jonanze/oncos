#   (board-maps/hr-mbc/independent-review.md, 40 findings). Draft 1 changed: HER2-ultralow on its own line with
#   chemotherapy first, licence and grade attachments corrected, ABC8 conditions written in, seven chips added, ・ lines

# Format and rules: see the header of maps/early-nsclc/map.txt
# Scope (Jonan 2026-11-14): the systemic course by line (first line, after first progression, then chemotherapy and
#   antibody-drug conjugates), with three small panels: bone-modifying agents, surgery of the primary tumour in de novo
#   stage IV disease, and oligometastatic disease. Brain metastases and palliative radiotherapy are out. Chips may cite
#   OlympiAD, EMBRACA and NRG BR002 from the shared sheet "General Principles", and SABR-COMET from "Supportive Care"
# Layout: the course carries "| parallel". Three presentations start side by side: first line in endocrine-sensitive
#   disease, first line after early relapse, and visceral crisis. All three lead into one step after first progression,
#   whose numbered lines are the populations by previous treatment and biomarker. Their order is the ESMO display order,
#   not a ranking. Two steps follow in time order: the first cytotoxic line, then treatment after chemotherapy. No
#   header step and no line labels.
#   Testing, the rules shared by every endocrine-based line and chemotherapy are bands. Eligibility criteria are written
#   into the numbered lines (Jonan 2026-11-14, no criteria table, no assay panel)
# One narrative: a point several guidelines agree on is one statement with all their tags. A ・ line gives a difference
#   (a cut-off, a line of treatment, a grade), a condition or a licence. One numbered line per population
# Guideline set (Jonan 2026-11-14): PAGA 2023, ESMO 2026 with Living Guideline v2.0, ASCO 2021 with rapid updates to
#   2024, NCCN 9.2026, and ABC8 2026 tagged only where it adds a point. JBCS is out. Tag order PAGA, ESMO, ASCO, NCCN, ABC8
# Tags give the grade as printed, evidence first and strength second, on the statement the guideline graded:
#   〔PAGA level, grade〕 Pan-Asian adapted ESMO guideline for metastatic breast cancer 2023 (Im, ESMO Open 2023;8:101541),
#     adapted from ESMO 2021. A bare 〔PAGA〕 marks body text, a figure footnote or an ungraded Table 1 row
#   〔ESMO level, grade〕 ESMO metastatic breast cancer guideline 2026 (de Azambuja, Ann Oncol 2026;37:1203-1219), read
#     from the Living Guideline v2.0 pages (August 2026). A bare 〔ESMO〕 marks an algorithm footnote or a printed order
#   〔ASCO quality, strength〕 ASCO endocrine and targeted therapy update 2021 (Burstein), chemotherapy and targeted therapy
#     update 2021 (Moy) and the rapid updates of 2022 (trastuzumab deruxtecan), 2023 (sacituzumab govitecan, ESR1) and
#     2024 (capivasertib). A bare 〔ASCO〕 marks a 2016 recommendation reprinted without a rating, a qualifying statement,
#     a table or the clinical interpretation
#   〔NCCN category〕 NCCN Breast Cancer 9.2026 (22 October 2026), with "preferred" as printed. A bare 〔NCCN〕 marks a
#     footnote, a principles page or the Discussion
#   〔ABC8 level/grade〕 ABC8 international consensus 2026 (Cardoso, Breast 2026;90:104921), LoE/GoR as printed, without
#     the consensus percentage. The two SERENA-6 grades were placed from the row image on PDF page 15
# Trial marks: + overall survival benefit, ~ surrogate endpoint or non-inferiority, - no benefit from the tested change,
#   * no comparison for the statement. Single-arm trials are not chips (Jonan), so TBCRC 048 and BYLieve stay off the map.
#   MF07-01 is marked - because its 36-month survival analysis was not improved (inclusion.txt, Result), with the later
#   survival gain left to Jonan
# Giredestrant with everolimus is a ※ line after first progression (ABC8 alone, Jonan 2026-11-14). Gedatolisib is in ⑥
#   after first progression (Jonan 2026-11-14)
# ※ lines for options that NCCN lists and no core guideline grades: camizestrant switch. Imlunestrant with
#   abemaciclib and vepdegestrant sit as ・ lines in the ESR1 class (header Rule ⑦)

band: Biopsy and testing
  ① A metastatic lesion is biopsied at the first diagnosis of metastatic disease, to confirm histology and reassess ER, PgR and HER2 〔PAGA I, B〕 〔ESMO I, A〕 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ・With discordant receptor results, treatment follows the biopsy of the recurrence 〔PAGA〕 〔ESMO V, B〕
    ・Endocrine therapy is considered when ER is positive in at least one biopsy 〔ABC8 Expert opinion/B〕
    ・When a biopsy cannot be obtained safely, treatment starts on the receptor status of the primary tumour 〔NCCN〕
  ※ ER-low disease (1 to 10%) is eligible for endocrine therapy 〔NCCN〕
    ・ER-low disease is not treated with endocrine therapy alone 〔ABC8 III/B〕
  ② HER2 is reassessed as low, ultralow or 0 before treatment that is not endocrine-based 〔ESMO I, A〕
    ・HER2-low is IHC 1+, or IHC 2+ with negative in situ hybridisation 〔PAGA〕 〔ASCO〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・HER2-ultralow is IHC 0 with faint, incomplete membrane staining in 10% of tumour cells or fewer 〔NCCN〕
    ・HER2-low status on one sample, from any stage of the disease, is sufficient for trastuzumab deruxtecan 〔ABC8 I/A〕
  ③ Germline BRCA1/2 status is tested in HER2-negative metastatic disease 〔PAGA I, A〕 〔ESMO I, A〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・Where treatment is available, germline PALB2 〔ESMO I, A〕 and somatic BRCA1/2 〔ESMO III, A〕 are also tested
  ④ PIK3CA 〔PAGA I, A〕, AKT1 and PTEN alterations are tested on tumour tissue or ctDNA, mainly in endocrine-resistant disease 〔ESMO I, A〕 〔ASCO High, Strong〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・When ctDNA shows no alteration, tumour tissue is tested 〔ASCO High, Strong〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・Tissue is preferred to ctDNA for homozygous loss of PTEN 〔NCCN〕
  ⑤ ESR1 mutations are tested at progression on endocrine therapy, with or without a CDK4/6 inhibitor 〔PAGA〕 〔ESMO I, A〕 〔ASCO High, Strong〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・ctDNA is the preferred sample 〔ASCO High, Strong〕 〔NCCN〕, with tissue testing when no mutation is detected 〔NCCN〕 〔ABC8 I/B〕
    ・ESR1 wild-type disease is tested again at later progression 〔ASCO High, Strong〕
  ※ Broader genomic profiling is limited to results that change treatment or give access to a trial 〔PAGA V, B〕 〔ESMO III, B〕

band: Endocrine-based therapy at every line
  ① Pre- and perimenopausal women: ovarian function suppression or ablation with every endocrine-based therapy 〔PAGA I, A〕 〔ESMO I, A〕 〔ASCO〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・Suppression starts with or before endocrine therapy 〔NCCN〕
    ・Tamoxifen alone is an option 〔PAGA〕 〔ASCO〕
    ・Tamoxifen alone is limited to women who decline ovarian suppression 〔ABC8 I/C〕
    ・On an aromatase inhibitor, oestradiol is measured serially, even with amenorrhoea 〔ABC8 Expert opinion/B〕
  ② Men: the same treatment as for women 〔NCCN〕, with a GnRH analogue when an aromatase inhibitor is given 〔ASCO High, Strong〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・An aromatase inhibitor without a GnRH analogue may be considered, with close monitoring of response 〔ABC8 IV/B〕
    ・Tamoxifen is a preferred agent for untreated disease or relapse after more than 12 months, and can be combined with abemaciclib or palbociclib 〔ABC8 IV/B〕
  ※ CDK4/6 inhibitors are as effective at 70 or older as in younger patients, and cause more fatigue, diarrhoea, neutropenia and hepatotoxicity at 75 or older 〔ASCO〕
    ・A G8 screen comes first, with a full geriatric assessment when the score is low 〔ABC8 I/A〕

band: Chemotherapy
  ① No need for a rapid response: single agents in sequence 〔PAGA II, A〕 〔ESMO II, A〕 〔ASCO Moderate, Strong〕 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ・The agents are an anthracycline, a taxane, capecitabine, eribulin, vinorelbine, gemcitabine or a platinum 〔PAGA〕 〔NCCN〕
    ・As the first chemotherapy, capecitabine or a taxane may be considered 〔ESMO I, C〕, and oral chemotherapy is generally preferred 〔NCCN〕
    ・An anthracycline or a taxane is the usual first chemotherapy when neither was given for early disease 〔ABC8 I/A〕
    ・An anthracycline or a taxane can be given again after a disease-free interval of 12 months or more 〔PAGA II, B〕 〔ESMO II, B〕
    ・Metronomic chemotherapy is an option when no rapid response is needed 〔ABC8 I/B〕
    + EMBRACE [t-embrace]
  ② Imminent organ failure: combination chemotherapy 〔PAGA II, A〕 〔ESMO V, A〕
    ・Combinations are also used for symptomatic, rapidly progressing or immediately life-threatening disease 〔ASCO Moderate, Strong〕 〔NCCN〕
  ※ Chemotherapy generally continues until progression or intolerable toxicity 〔PAGA I, B〕 〔ASCO〕 〔NCCN 2A〕, within the cumulative dose limit for anthracyclines 〔PAGA〕
    + Gennari 2011 [t-gennari-et-al]
  ※ Bevacizumab may be added to capecitabine or a taxane in the first chemotherapy line 〔PAGA I, C〕 〔ESMO I, C〕, and in the second 〔PAGA〕
    ・Bevacizumab has no EMA or FDA licence in this line 〔ESMO〕
  ※ Dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase testing precedes capecitabine 〔ESMO V, A〕
    ・Testing may be considered before treatment or after unexpected toxicity, since the deficiency is rare in Asian countries 〔PAGA II, D〕

  Visceral crisis at presentation
    branch: VC
    ① Chemotherapy 〔PAGA III, B〕 〔ESMO V, B〕 〔ASCO〕 〔NCCN 2A〕, or endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor in certain circumstances 〔NCCN〕 〔ABC8 II/B〕
      ・In liver visceral crisis, weekly chemotherapy at lower doses is recommended 〔ABC8 IV/B〕
      ・With bone marrow infiltration, weekly reduced-dose paclitaxel, capecitabine, or endocrine therapy with a CDK4/6 inhibitor are among the best options 〔ABC8 IV/B〕
      ・After response or stabilisation, treatment can change to endocrine therapy 〔PAGA〕 〔NCCN〕, with a CDK4/6 inhibitor when none was given 〔PAGA〕 〔ABC8 Expert opinion/B〕
    ※ Visceral crisis is severe organ dysfunction with rapid progression, and not the presence of visceral metastases alone 〔NCCN〕
      ・Examples are bilirubin rising rapidly above 1.5 times the upper limit of normal without biliary obstruction, and dyspnoea at rest that pleural drainage does not relieve 〔ABC8 Expert opinion/NA〕
    ※ Extensive or symptomatic visceral disease without organ failure is treated with endocrine therapy and a CDK4/6 inhibitor in preference to chemotherapy 〔NCCN〕 〔ABC8 I/A〕

    branch: CYT
