# Format and rules: see the header of maps/early-nsclc/map.txt
#   scale: Low | ...   the risk ramp, the only colour on any map: tints the adjuvant grid rows, hues their
#                   label and treatment cells, and rules the ※ and ① lines of that tier (Jonan 2026-11-09)
#   ・ text          one statement under the line above (a guideline's position, a licence, a condition)
#
# Rules (Jonan 2026-11-08/09/10; node maps/check.cjs enforces the starred ones)
#   Layout fits the disease: a course from diagnosis to recurrence and later lines
#   Square lines only; no result colours outside the risk ramp
# * Every sheet row is on the map or under off: with a reason
# * Trial lines cite clinical trials, pooled analyses of trials or meta-analyses only
#   Inclusion: maps/inclusion.txt; GOG-86P kept under ⑥d and ⑥e, MITO END-2 under ⑥d (Jonan 2026-11-10)
#   Standard options only; an EU or US licensed option that a guideline does not list stays on the
#     line with its licence noted and says which guidelines leave it out
#   Option order follows the guidelines' preference; where they disagree a ・ line names each
#   East Asian guidelines come first in Jonan's practice (2026-11-10): a JSGO or KSGO option used in Japan or
#     Korea is a numbered option with its tag, never a ※ line or under off:
#   One statement per line, no semicolon chains: a guideline's position, a licence or a condition goes on a
#     ・ line under the line it qualifies (Jonan 2026-11-10)
# * Plain clinical wording
#   Tags give evidence first, strength second, as printed, on the statement the guideline graded:
#     〔ESGO level, grade〕 ESGO-ESTRO-ESP endometrial carcinoma guidelines, update 2025 (Concin)
#     〔NCCN category, preference〕 NCCN Uterine Neoplasms 3.2026 (16 June 2026): ENDO-1 to ENDO-10, ENDO-A,
#       ENDO-C and ENDO-D; category 2A unless stated; NCCN stages by FIGO 2009 and chooses adjuvant treatment
#       by stage and grade, not by molecular class; options NCCN alone lists sit on ※ lines
#     〔JSGO evidence, strength〕 Japan Society of Gynecologic Oncology 2023 guidelines for uterine body
#       neoplasm, English version (J Gynecol Oncol 2025;36:e119); evidence A to D, strength 1 recommend or
#       2 suggest, "against" for a recommendation not to; tagged only where JSGO differs from ESGO and NCCN
#       (adjuvant chemotherapy, lymphadenectomy); its literature ends in 2021, before chemo-immunotherapy
#   KSGO 5.1 (2025) read and left off: it grades only first-line chemo-immunotherapy and durvalumab with
#     olaparib, which NCCN already lists (Jonan 2026-11-11)
#   ESMO 2022 (Oaknin) and PAGA 2023 dropped as stale: no living update, both predate RUBY, NRG-GY018 and
#     DUO-E (Jonan 2026-11-11); SGO 2026 not used

map: Endometrial Cancer
source: ESGO [t-esgo-estro-esp-endometrial-carcinoma-management-guidelines]
source: NCCN {url https://www.nccn.org/guidelines/category_1}

scale: Low | Intermediate | High-intermediate | High

band: Diagnosis and surgery
  ① Total hysterectomy and bilateral salpingo-oophorectomy, by minimally invasive surgery 〔ESGO I, A〕 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ・Sentinel node biopsy for staging 〔ESGO I, A〕 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
    ・JSGO: pelvic lymphadenectomy when intermediate or high risk is presumed 〔JSGO B, 1〕
    ・JSGO: para-aortic lymphadenectomy to the renal vein as well 〔JSGO C, 2〕
    ・JSGO: sentinel node biopsy in place of lymphadenectomy only within a clinical trial 〔JSGO B, 2〕
  ※ Infracolic omentectomy for serous carcinoma, carcinosarcoma and undifferentiated carcinoma 〔ESGO IV, B〕
  ② Unfit for surgery: definitive radiotherapy 〔ESGO II, B〕 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
    ・EBRT with image-guided brachytherapy for high-grade or deeply invasive tumours 〔ESGO II, B〕
    ・Brachytherapy alone for low-grade tumours without deep invasion 〔ESGO II, B〕
    ・NCCN: a levonorgestrel IUD or other hormonal therapy in selected patients 〔NCCN 2A〕
  ③ Molecular class and ER in every tumour, on the biopsy where possible 〔ESGO IV, A〕
    ・NCCN: POLE, MMR and p53 in every tumour 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ・NCCN: ER and PR in stage III–IV and recurrence 〔NCCN 2A〕
  ④ MMR immunohistochemistry in every tumour, which also screens for Lynch syndrome 〔ESGO III, A〕 〔NCCN 2A〕
  ⑤ HER2 in advanced or recurrent p53 abnormal tumours, and in all serous carcinomas and carcinosarcomas 〔ESGO IV, C〕
    ・NCCN: HER2 in every p53 abnormal tumour, and in all metastatic or recurrent disease 〔NCCN 2A〕
  ⑥ Metastatic or recurrent disease: TMB, and NTRK and RET fusions 〔NCCN 2A〕
  ※ NCCN 3.2026 stages by FIGO 2009, ESGO 2025 by FIGO 2023 with molecular class

course:
  Stage III–IV at diagnosis
    ① Complete resection feasible: cytoreduction, removing only suspicious nodes 〔ESGO IV, B〕 〔NCCN 2A〕
      ・NCCN: preoperative systemic therapy is an alternative 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ② Unresectable from local extent: definitive radiotherapy with image-guided brachytherapy, or systemic therapy first, chosen by molecular class 〔ESGO IV, C〕
      ・NCCN: EBRT with or without brachytherapy and systemic therapy, or systemic therapy, then reassess for surgery 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ③ Disseminated disease, or residual disease after surgery: first-line systemic therapy 〔NCCN 2A〕
    next: Advanced or recurrent, first line
  Newly diagnosed, after hysterectomy and staging
    Classify in this order: POLE, then MMR, then p53. With more than one feature, POLE outranks MMR and MMR outranks p53 〔ESGO IV, B〕
    Tumours with none of these are NSMP, split by ER (positive if 10% or more) and grade
    | Risk group | POLE mutated | MMR deficient | NSMP, ER positive, low grade | p53 abnormal, or NSMP with ER negative or high grade | Adjuvant treatment |
    | Low | Stage I–II | Low grade with invasion under 50%, or high grade without invasion | Myometrial invasion under 50% | - | None 〔ESGO II, A〕 |
    | Intermediate | - | Myometrial invasion 50% or more, or high grade with any invasion | Myometrial invasion 50% or more, or cervical stromal invasion | - | Vaginal brachytherapy 〔ESGO I, A〕 |
    | High-intermediate | - | Substantial LVSI, or cervical stromal invasion | Substantial LVSI | - | Pelvic EBRT 〔ESGO II, A〕 |
    | High | - | Stage III–IVA | Stage III–IVA | Any myometrial invasion, stage I–IVA | Chemoradiotherapy 〔ESGO I, A〕 |
    | Uncertain | Stage III–IVA | - | - | No myometrial invasion | POLE mutated: less than high-risk treatment can be considered 〔ESGO IV, B〕. No invasion: usually none 〔ESGO IV, C〕 |
    ※ Risk groups use FIGO 2023 stage with molecular class
    ※ Intermediate: no adjuvant treatment is an option, especially under 60 years or in low-grade tumours 〔ESGO III, C〕
    ※ High-intermediate: vaginal brachytherapy instead, especially if node-negative 〔ESGO II, B〕
      ・No adjuvant treatment if node-negative, low grade and without substantial LVSI 〔ESGO IV, B〕
    ※ High: sequential chemotherapy and radiotherapy, or chemotherapy with or without vaginal brachytherapy, are alternatives 〔ESGO I, B〕
    ※ NCCN chooses by FIGO 2009 stage and grade in endometrioid carcinoma, not by molecular class
      ・Stage IA, grade 1–2: observation preferred 〔NCCN 2A〕
      ・Stage IA grade 3, or IB grade 1–2: vaginal brachytherapy preferred 〔NCCN 2A〕
      ・Stage IB grade 3: EBRT or vaginal brachytherapy 〔NCCN 2A〕, with or without systemic therapy 〔NCCN 2B〕
      ・Stage II: EBRT preferred, with or without vaginal brachytherapy 〔NCCN 2A〕, with or without systemic therapy 〔NCCN 2B〕
      ・Stage III–IV: systemic therapy, with or without EBRT or vaginal brachytherapy 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ※ High, MMR deficient stage III–IVA: chemotherapy with a checkpoint inhibitor, with or without EBRT 〔ESGO II, B〕
      ・NCCN: pembrolizumab with chemotherapy for stage III–IVA with measurable disease after surgery, or stage IVB 〔NCCN 1, preferred〕
      ・NCCN: dostarlimab with chemotherapy also without measurable disease in stage IIIC2–IV 〔NCCN 1, preferred〕
      ・NCCN: pembrolizumab with chemotherapy should be considered in MMR deficient stage III–IV outside the NRG-GY018 criteria, citing KEYNOTE-B21
      - KEYNOTE-B21 | DFS HR 1.02, MMR deficient HR 0.31
      ※ Pembrolizumab with adjuvant chemotherapy did not improve DFS in all patients
      ・The ESGO and NCCN statements rest on the MMR deficient subgroup
    ① Intermediate risk: vaginal brachytherapy controls the vagina as well as pelvic EBRT, with fewer bowel effects
      ~ PORTEC-1 | Locoregional recurrence 4% vs 14%
      ~ GOG 99 | Recurrence 3% vs 12%
      ※ Neither trial improved overall survival
      ~ PORTEC-2 | Vaginal recurrence 1.8% vs 1.6%
    ② High-intermediate risk: pelvic EBRT
      - GOG 249 | RFS HR 0.92
      ※ Vaginal brachytherapy with chemotherapy was not better and left more nodal relapses
    ③ High risk: chemoradiotherapy improved survival over radiotherapy alone, most in stage III and serous carcinoma
      ・NCCN: cisplatin with RT, then carboplatin-paclitaxel 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
      + PORTEC-3 | OS HR 0.70
      + PORTEC-3 molecular | p53 abnormal OS HR 0.55
      ※ Chemotherapy added nothing in MMR deficient tumours
      ・ESGO 2025 judged this too little evidence to omit it in stage III
      - GOG 258 | RFS HR 0.90
      ※ Chemotherapy alone gave the same relapse-free survival as chemoradiotherapy, with more pelvic and nodal relapses
    ④ Chemotherapy alone, by JSGO pathological risk group
      ・High risk: doxorubicin with cisplatin 〔JSGO A, 1〕, or paclitaxel with carboplatin 〔JSGO B, 2〕
      ・Intermediate risk: the same regimens 〔JSGO C, 2〕
      ・Low risk: no chemotherapy 〔JSGO C, 1 against〕
      ・Pelvic radiotherapy is an option to reduce pelvic recurrence 〔JSGO C, 2〕
      - JGOG 2033 {pmid 17996926} | PFS at 5 years 81.8% vs 83.5%
      ※ Chemotherapy and pelvic radiotherapy gave the same PFS and OS in all patients
      ※ In an unplanned high-intermediate subgroup, chemotherapy gave longer PFS and OS
      - JGOG 2043 {pmid 30896757} | PFS at 5 years 73.3% vs 79.0% vs 73.9%
      ※ Doxorubicin with cisplatin, docetaxel with cisplatin and paclitaxel with carboplatin gave the same PFS and OS
      ※ Paclitaxel with carboplatin is the more common choice in Japan, doxorubicin with cisplatin about 10%
    next: Recurrence | Recurrence
  Recurrence
    Vaginal, pelvic or in a few sites: local treatment first
    ① No radiotherapy before: EBRT with image-guided brachytherapy, with or without chemotherapy 〔ESGO IV, A〕
      ・NCCN: EBRT with or without brachytherapy 〔NCCN 2A〕, with or without systemic therapy 〔NCCN 2B〕
      ・NCCN: or surgical resection 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ② Vaginal brachytherapy only before: EBRT with a brachytherapy boost 〔ESGO IV, C〕
    ③ EBRT before: radical surgery if clear margins are feasible 〔ESGO IV, A〕
      ・NCCN: or systemic therapy, or brachytherapy for small-volume vaginal disease 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ※ MMR deficient, no checkpoint inhibitor before: chemotherapy with a checkpoint inhibitor, then the checkpoint inhibitor alone 〔ESGO II, B〕
    ④ One to five metastases in up to three regions: surgery, stereotactic radiotherapy or ablation 〔ESGO IV, B〕
      ・NCCN: resection or EBRT or SBRT 〔NCCN 2A〕, ablation 〔NCCN 2B〕
    Widespread, or not suited to local treatment: first-line systemic therapy
    next: Advanced or recurrent, first line | Widespread
  Advanced or recurrent, first line
    Choose by MMR status. Six cycles of carboplatin-paclitaxel is the standard chemotherapy 〔ESGO I, A〕
    ① MMR deficient: carboplatin-paclitaxel with dostarlimab, durvalumab or pembrolizumab, then the antibody as maintenance 〔ESGO I, A〕 〔NCCN 1, preferred〕
      ・NCCN: not pembrolizumab in carcinosarcoma
      ~ RUBY | MMR deficient PFS HR 0.28
      ~ NRG-GY018 [t-nrg-gy018-keynote-868] | MMR deficient PFS HR 0.30
      ~ DUO-E [t-duo-e] | MMR deficient PFS HR 0.42
    ② MMR proficient: carboplatin-paclitaxel 〔ESGO I, A〕 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
      ・ESGO: adding pembrolizumab or dostarlimab can be considered 〔ESGO I, B〕
      ・NCCN: adding pembrolizumab or dostarlimab 〔NCCN 1, preferred〕
      ・Durvalumab with chemotherapy, then durvalumab with olaparib 〔ESGO I, B〕 〔NCCN 2B〕
      ~ RUBY | All patients PFS HR 0.64, OS HR 0.64 at first interim, boundary not met
      ~ NRG-GY018 [t-nrg-gy018-keynote-868] | MMR proficient PFS HR 0.54
      ~ DUO-E [t-duo-e] | MMR proficient, with olaparib, PFS HR 0.57
    ③ HER2 3+, when a checkpoint inhibitor cannot be given: carboplatin-paclitaxel with trastuzumab 〔ESGO II, B〕
      ・NCCN: HER2 positive serous carcinoma or carcinosarcoma, with no checkpoint inhibitor condition 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
      + Fader et al. | OS HR 0.58
      ※ Fader et al. enrolled serous carcinoma only
    ④ Low grade, ER positive, low volume or slowly growing: a progestin, medroxyprogesterone or megestrol 〔ESGO III, A〕 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
      ・An aromatase inhibitor or tamoxifen are alternatives 〔ESGO IV, C〕 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
      ・NCCN: megestrol alternating with tamoxifen, everolimus with letrozole, or fulvestrant 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕
    ※ NCCN alone: a CDK4/6 inhibitor with letrozole or fulvestrant in ER positive tumours 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
    ※ MMR proficient relapse after adjuvant chemotherapy, when chemotherapy cannot be given: lenvatinib with pembrolizumab can be considered 〔ESGO III, C〕
      ・NCCN: after any platinum, including adjuvant, with no condition on chemotherapy 〔NCCN 1, useful in certain circumstances〕
    ※ Carboplatin-paclitaxel replaced doxorubicin, cisplatin and paclitaxel
      ~ GOG 209 | Non-inferior, OS HR 1.002
    ※ Bevacizumab added to carboplatin-paclitaxel did not improve PFS. Not licensed
      ・ESGO 2025 does not list it
      ・NCCN: preferred in stage III–IV 〔NCCN 2A, preferred〕, other recommended at recurrence 〔NCCN 2A, other recommended〕
      ・In exploratory analyses of GOG-86P, TP53-mutated or p53-overexpressing tumours did better with bevacizumab
      - GOG-86P [t-gog-86p] | PFS HR 0.81 against a historical control
      - MITO END-2 | PFS 13.7 vs 10.5 months, HR 0.84
    next: Progression after platinum | Progression
  Progression after platinum
    Repeat MMR testing on the relapse when feasible 〔ESGO IV, B〕 〔NCCN 2A〕
    ① MMR deficient, no checkpoint inhibitor before: pembrolizumab or dostarlimab alone 〔ESGO III, A〕 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
      ・Lenvatinib with pembrolizumab is an option 〔ESGO I, B〕
      * KEYNOTE-158 [t-keynote-158--endometrial-cancer] | Response
      * GARNET | Response
    ※ NCCN alone: avelumab, nivolumab or tislelizumab for MMR deficient tumours 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
      ・Retifanlimab 〔NCCN 2B〕
    ② MMR proficient, no checkpoint inhibitor before: lenvatinib with pembrolizumab 〔ESGO I, A〕 〔NCCN 1, useful in certain circumstances〕
      + KEYNOTE-775 [t-keynote-775-study-309] | OS HR
    ※ NCCN alone: axitinib with avelumab for MMR proficient tumours 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
    ※ NCCN alone: TMB-high tumours, pembrolizumab, or ipilimumab with nivolumab 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
    ③ After a checkpoint inhibitor: platinum rechallenge, doxorubicin, weekly paclitaxel or endocrine therapy 〔ESGO IV, B〕
      ・NCCN: single agents or doublets such as cisplatin-doxorubicin, doxorubicin or paclitaxel 〔NCCN 2A, other recommended〕
    ④ HER2 2+ or 3+: trastuzumab deruxtecan 〔ESGO III, B〕 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
      ・NCCN: in carcinosarcoma also HER2 1+ 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
      * DESTINY-PanTumor02 [t-destiny-pantumor02] | IHC 3+ response 84.6%
      ※ HER2 3+ with platinum rechallenge possible: carboplatin-paclitaxel with trastuzumab 〔ESGO II, B〕
    ※ NCCN alone: NTRK fusion, larotrectinib, entrectinib or repotrectinib. RET fusion, selpercatinib 〔NCCN 2A, useful in certain circumstances〕
